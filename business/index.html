A real example

Timesheets on the software they already had

A Birmingham children's charity needed one reliable record of everyone's hours, with no new system to log into and no new licence cost. I built it on Microsoft 365: simple forms for staff, and a report for managers that updates itself.

It's in trial now, and I'll say plainly that my published example so far is a charity. The problem is common in small businesses too: hours, orders or jobs recorded in several places, and someone adding them up by hand.

How AI helped

I used Claude to speed up the building and the debugging. I set the requirements, checked every change and made the calls on what went live.

Questions I get asked

What does it cost?

It depends on the job, so there is no price list. After the free call I give you a fixed price in writing before any work starts.

Do I need to know anything about AI?

No. Come with the problem, not the technology.

Is our data safe?

I set the data rules at the start: what can go into AI tools, what can't, and who has access. I flag the questions to put to whoever looks after your data protection. I don't give legal advice.

Will it replace my staff?

No. The aim is less typing and chasing, so people have more time for customers and the work only people can do.

What if it doesn't work for us?

I'll tell you before you spend anything, and I'll say if the cheaper answer is no AI at all.
