A real job

Timesheets for a Birmingham children's charity

They have fewer than 25 staff and needed a reliable record of hours worked, including for people whose contracted hours change between term time and school holidays. The rules: no new system to log into, no new licence cost, and something their own team could run.

My first idea was an Excel file per person. It worked for two test users and fell over when a manager needed everyone's hours in one place. So I rebuilt it on what they already pay for: three SharePoint lists, a simple Power Apps form where each person sees only their own hours, and an Excel report that updates itself and flags anyone over or under.

It's in trial now. Getting the report to refresh reliably took more work than I'd planned for, and I'd say so on any similar job.

How AI helped

Building it Claude

I used Claude to speed up the building and the debugging. I set the requirements, checked every change and made the calls on what went live.

Reporting and analysis Copilot, not live yet

The charity and I are now looking at how to use Microsoft Copilot to help analyse the timesheet data and produce the reports. That isn't live yet.

How I work

Most advice is written for businesses, not charities

You hold beneficiary and safeguarding information. Trustees are accountable. Funders want reports. There is no budget for mistakes. I start from those constraints, not from the tool.

If it doesn't save time or money, don't do it

Before anything is built I tell you what it will cost to run, who can see the data, and what happens if it breaks.

Someone has to own it afterwards

Every tool I build has a named owner, a written handover and a plain guide. If nobody can look after it, I build something simpler.

It depends on the job, so there is no price list. After the free call I give you a fixed price in writing before any work starts. Everyone in the third sector pays the same rate, whatever the size of the organisation.

Do I need to know anything about AI?

No. Come with the problem, not the technology.

Is our data safe?

I set the data rules at the start: what can go into AI tools, what can't, and who has access. I flag the questions to put to your data protection lead. I don't give legal advice.

Will it replace our staff or volunteers?

No. The aim is less typing and chasing, so people have more time for the work only people can do.

What if it doesn't work for us?

I'll tell you before you spend anything, and I'll say if the cheaper answer is no AI at all.
